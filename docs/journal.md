### 2026-11-20: the telescope on the home network (bench only, no frames)

No sky, no frames. The aim was to be on the internet and reach the telescope at the same time.

- The home router (Xfinity) used `10.0.0.x`. Station mode joined the Wi-Fi and then the Seestar app said "network connect failed". ZWO support says on its forum that it restricts `10.0.0.x` for station mode, because the telescope's own Wi-Fi uses that range.
- The owner moved the router to `192.168.1.x` (the router's admin page, Gateway, Connection, Local IP Network). The telescope was reset, set up in station mode again, and the Alpaca switch turned on in the app. It joined at `192.168.1.200`.
- From the Mac: port 32323 answered, the Alpaca device list had the telescope, two cameras, two focusers, the filter wheel and a switch, `seestar.local` resolved to the same address, and ping had no loss (about 48 ms). Open-Meteo and the Harvard VizieR mirror both returned HTTP 200 from the same computer.
- `python main.py --hardware-check --no-park` passed, once with the IP address and once with `seestar.local` as the host: connect, site match (about 0 km), focuser read (1222 of 0 to 2600). Warnings that already existed: the mount reports JNow coordinates, the site-elevation write is not implemented by the driver, and the focuser step size is not implemented. The mount reported altitude -89.8 and was not tracking.
- The router would not let the owner reserve the telescope's address, so the host stays `seestar.local`. The address may change.
- Not seen: a mission over station mode, and whether the address and the Alpaca switch survive a power cycle.
- Decision (owner): Astronomo moves forward without an MPC observatory code. It only matters for asteroid astrometry and stays an option in [Phase 7.4](../ROADMAP.md#phase-7-reach).
- Decision (owner): `asteroid_finder` and `rubin_followup` are kept and marked drafts, not deleted. They stay tested and run in simulation and on archive replays, but are refused on a real telescope unless `ASTRONOMO_ALLOW_DRAFT_PLUGINS=1` is set. The default plugin is now `variable_stars`.

The 2026-11-13 capture does not count. It stopped at 32 of 480 frames, before ingress, through a tree, and before the setup checks.

The first saved plan, run on 2026-11-15, does not count either. Plan #4 (HD 189733, mission `m_20261116_005709_bf75`) passed the wait because the window was already open (2026-11-16T00:51:26Z), and the visit started. The live weather call failed. The command had `ASTRONOMO_ASSUME_CLEAR` set; the log shows the gate used the forecast saved at 2026-11-16T00:00Z, which read clear. The telescope slewed and took all 40 frames. Gaia could not be reached, so the stars were not matched and the target was not found in the frames. No magnitude was measured. The plan was marked DONE.

Those frames were plate-solved the next day, on the computer, with Astrometry.net 0.97 (`solve-field`, Tycho-2 indexes 4107–4112). Frame 1 and frame 2 both locked. The camera centre was RA 299.54°, Dec +24.04°, about 87 arcmin from the command (RA 300.179°, Dec +22.711°). HD 189733 lands off the chip, near pixel (−1077, 2817), so the 40 frames are a different field and are not reduced as that star. The measured scale is 2.24 arcsec/pixel, a field of about 81 by 143 arcmin. Up is about 136° west of north, and the image is mirrored. The header said `PIXSCALE` 1.195, because a 2160×3840 frame was treated as half the S50's 2.39. A search around 1.195 matches nothing. New frames are written and searched at 2.39 arcsec/pixel; 2.24 sits inside the 0.8–1.25 window (1.91–2.99). No live slew has used that scale yet.

Two more variable-star visits followed on 2026-11-16 (local evening), both V0381 And, both finished on the telescope:

With a wider finder and rotation correction the same frames give a magnitude of 7.111 ± 0.060 (`python main.py --reanalyze m_20261117_005511_cccc`). The star's identity was confirmed by Gaia 1.1 arcsec from the catalogue position. The pipeline calls this "brighter than range" (catalogue V 7.34); the zero point is Gaia G, not V.

The ESA archive returned HTTP 408. The magnitude is the same Gaia DR3 table from the Harvard VizieR mirror.

## Making the data usable: findings from the V0381 And and Hat-P-23 runs

From the two V0381 And runs on 2026-11-16. The scatter on a good night was 48 ppt with poor focus, and the next night's data could not be trusted. In this order, because step 1 decides how much the later ones matter:

Built and tested in code on 2026-11-18, before the next real run. "Built" means the tests pass and the tools were tried on the two stored nights. It does not mean the sky has confirmed them. Each item says what was found, and what is still a default to be confirmed.

- [x] **1. Find out why every star swung up to 35% between frames.** `core/frame_watch.py` measures, for every frame, the summed flux of the first frame's brightest stars, the sky level, the star width and the drift. `--reanalyze` prints and plots them (`frame_stats.json`, `frame_stats.png`). On the 40-frame night it showed a smooth fade: sky and star flux fell together by about 40%, with no change in star width. That is the signature of thin cloud or dew, not of tracking. The 12-frame night is cloud plus field rotation near the zenith. A swing in the light curve of up to 35% was transparency, not noise.
- [x] **1a. A per-frame view while the sequence runs.** After every frame the watcher prints one console line, writes a row to the mission log, saves `frame_stats.json` and a latest-frame preview, and the dashboard shows it beside the first-frame view. It costs about 2 s a frame (11 s on the first), inside the 20 s gap between frames. The dashboard section refreshes itself (`st.fragment(run_every=...)`), so no click is needed. Switch it off with `ASTRONOMO_FRAME_WATCH=0`. It warns when the flux or sky level changes by more than `ASTRONOMO_FRAME_WATCH_WARN_FRACTION` (0.20) from a rolling median, and for a slow fade over many frames. Seen on 2026-11-18. On plan #19 the field drifted past the 120 px match window, the watch reported the stars gone, and the faint-percentages were meaningless while the sky stayed steady. On plan #22 it followed all 12 stars: flux 0.91 to 1.00 of frame 1, sky unchanged, no warning. Not in scope: a real video stream, which needs Seestar protocol work nobody has confirmed and would show nothing the per-frame numbers do not.
- [ ] **2. Run a clean 40-frame visit.** This one is yours. A target well away from the zenith (under about 70 degrees altitude), a sky confirmed clear, and a weather lookup that works. Done when the light-curve scatter is measured on a night with none of those excuses
- [x] **3. Keep targets away from the zenith.** `ASTRONOMO_MAX_ALT_DEG` (default 70) is now a site limit beside the minimum altitude. The mission interlock refuses a target above it, the Opportunities pages for transits and variable stars offer no window above it (`ASTRONOMO_VARSTAR_MAX_ALT_DEG` and `ASTRONOMO_TRANSIT_MAX_ALT_DEG` can only lower it), and the first-light picker uses it. V0381 And at 81 degrees is now refused.
- [x] **4. Stop a failed weather lookup from hiding a bad sky.** The watcher is the transparency check from the frames, and it works without the weather service. On the 40-frame night it flagged the 40% fade. Whether the warning is loud enough in a real run is still to be seen.
- [ ] **5. Focus slightly soft on purpose.** Built but **off by default**: `ASTRONOMO_FOCUS_TARGET_FWHM_PX` (or `--focus-target`) settles where the fitted focus curve reaches that width, approached from below. The sweep minimum on the first night was 2.19 px. Nobody has yet compared the scatter at the sharp and the soft focus on the same field, and SeeVar, which was reviewed for this, does not defocus either. Done when that comparison is made. Try 3.5 on the clean visit, then again at 0
- [x] **6. Reduce the zero-point scatter.** Done with a colour term: the zero point is fitted against the comparison stars' Gaia BP-RP, and the target's own colour is applied. The slope was -0.33 and -0.67 mag per mag on the two nights. The scatter fell from 0.14 to 0.04 mag on the good night, and the night magnitude moved from 7.11 to 7.32 ± 0.04, close to Gaia's mean G of 7.33. `ASTRONOMO_VARSTAR_COLOUR_TERM=off` turns it off. The Bayer pattern was inferred from the star fluxes: green is at (0,0) and (1,1), a GRBG-type layout (SeeVar's code says GRBG for its camera too). But measuring on the green sites alone was worse than the 2x2 average (scatter 158 to 273 ppt on the sharp night, and no comparison stars at all on the soft one), so the green extraction was removed. `BAYERPAT` stays unset in the file, and row 19 stays open.
- [x] **7. Correct the slow trend without flat frames.** Tried and **removed**. A flat built from the night's own drifting frames varies by only 2% across the chip, and applying it did not touch the trend. The trend on the 40-frame V0381 night is still there: the target gained 0.15 mag on its comparison stars over the sequence. Brighter stars faded less than fainter ones. Plan #22 showed it again on a steady sky: +0.12 mag across 12 frames while the frame watch held the bright stars between 0.91 and 1.00 and the sky did not move. The plugin reports the trend in the findings. Done when a clean 40-frame night shows whether it recurs
- [ ] **8. Centre the target.** Built but **off by default**: `ASTRONOMO_POINTING_RECENTRE_ARCMIN` (set it to, say, 10) makes one correcting slew when the target is on the chip but further from the centre than that. It is off because the Seestar's goto error is random, so a correcting slew can land as far away as the first, and SeeVar reports that over-correcting hurt (it accepts a target anywhere on the frame, with a 250 px margin). Turn it on only if a night shows an off-centre start losing the target from the chip

### Phase 2: Unattended and trustworthy

**Goal:** a mission can run without someone watching it, and its science claims survive scrutiny. Still no LLM changes.

- **Plans and the scheduler: what is built, and what waits.** The build order is *interlocks, then the manual hand-off and plan life cycle, then the scheduler after the first real run.* A scheduler that starts missions by itself is the one step that should not come first, because it removes the person who would catch a bad start.
  - [x] **Manual hand-off (built).** `python main.py --from-plan ID` builds a mission from a plan. Before it starts, the plan is re-checked (window still open, schedule moved on the cached lists, Sun/altitude/Moon across the window) and the person is told what changed. A closed window refuses the start and marks the plan `MISSED`. The mission observes exactly the planned target (`plan_id` in the mission state), not the plugin's own list. On a mock telescope it is a **rehearsal** and leaves the plan untouched; only a real-telescope run changes it.
  - [x] **Plan life cycle (built).** `PLANNED` to `STARTED` (linked to its mission) to `DONE` or `MISSED` (with a one-sentence outcome), or `PLANNED` to `MISSED` when the window passes unstarted, or `CANCELLED`. Shown on the Planned tab (with the exact commands) and on the mission page ("Started from plan #n").
  - [x] **Scheduler / daemon (built and tested in simulation, 2026-11-14). It does not start a night by itself.** `python main.py --schedule` lists plans whose windows are open and resumes a mission whose weather wait has passed, so that wait no longer holds a process. A plan starts only with `--confirm ID` (repeat it for more than one). `--once` looks once and exits; without it, the command polls. Starting on its own is `ASTRONOMO_SELF_START`, off by default, and the code refuses it unless `ASTRONOMO_UNATTENDED_CONFIRMED` is also set. That second flag is the record that weather, Sun, Moon, altitude and safe parking have been seen on this mount. Nothing here sets it, and how early a start must be is still what the first real night measures. `python main.py --stop` parks the telescope and leaves the mission stopped, so it will not resume. A telescope error already parks. An interrupt still checkpoints the mission and says to run the same command again.
  - [x] **Plans on the dashboard, and a start from there (built 2026-11-15; a real start from the button has not been used on the telescope).** The Plans page and the rail list every saved plan, whichever kind of observation was open when it was saved. Each row shows the target, the kind, the window, the status (`PLANNED`, `STARTED`, `DONE`, `MISSED`) and the mission once the plan has one. Rehearse and Start run `python main.py --from-plan`. The plan is re-checked there (window, moved schedule, Sun, altitude and Moon), a closed window is refused and marked `MISSED`, a rehearsal (`--simulate --fast`) leaves the plan as it was, and a real start is a separate confirmation that sets `ASTRONOMO_MOCK_MODE=false`. The page does not start a plan on its own, and this is not self-start: a person chose that plan and confirmed it. The button calls the hand-off. It does not talk to the telescope, and it cannot skip an interlock. The card shows the process it started and `python main.py --stop`, which parks the telescope and leaves the mission stopped. The Planned tab on Opportunities stays, for choosing a window. The command-line hand-off stays, for a terminal and for the first-light night.

- [x] **Reschedule on weather (built and tested in simulation, 2026-11-14).** When weather blocks a target past `max_sleep_cycles`, the mission keeps a wake time and the process exits, instead of aborting. The wake time is the next clear hour in the saved forecast, or one sleep interval when the file has none. `--schedule` resumes it. A target whose window ends before that hour is skipped. Cloud, wind and rain cover the whole site, so the mission is not rotated onto another target while it is cloudy. Site limits that never clear still end the mission, because waiting will not lift them.
- [ ] **Retry buttons: a mission must not sit in limbo because an outside service failed.** Today a call to a third party that fails mid-mission can leave a mission half-finished while the data to finish it is already on disk. On 2026-11-18 plan #22 ended that way: the 12 frames were saved, the live reduction could not resolve `vizier.cfa.harvard.edu`, the AAVSO file was `held`, and the magnitude appeared only after `python main.py --reanalyze` was run by hand later on another network. The goal is that a failed outside call is a visible, recoverable state, and that a mission can be analysed whenever the data can eventually be fetched. Nothing here changes what is sent: a person still decides, and a held file is never sent by a retry.
  - *Name the state.* Record on the mission which outside call failed (the service, what it was for, when, the error), and show it as its own state ("waiting on Gaia", "waiting on the forecast") instead of a generic hold. It is saved with the mission, so it survives a restart.
  - *A Retry button on the mission page, and `python main.py --retry MISSION_ID` for a terminal.* It repeats only the failed step, from the saved frames and data. It never touches the telescope and never re-takes frames, and it is not offered for a step that needs the mount. It builds on `--reanalyze`, the node retries (`ASTRONOMO_NODE_RETRIES`) and the `held` manifest status.
  - *A retry that works finishes the same mission.* Its results and files update in place, the file takes the status its normal rules give it (for example `held` to `draft`), and the log says it was a retry. A retry that fails again keeps the state and the new error. A person presses the button; nothing retries forever on its own.
  - *Done when:* in simulation, a mission whose Gaia, VizieR or Open-Meteo call fails ends in the named waiting state, the Retry button completes it once the service answers, and a test covers both. On a real night, switch the computer's network off during the reduction on a rehearsal, switch it back on, press Retry, and write down what happened.
- [ ] **Multi day missions.** Depends on the scheduler. Watch checkpoint growth (`observations` accumulates in state) and move bulky data out of state if it gets large.
- [ ] **Credible "unidentified".** Consult SkyBoT and MPC, link visits across nights, and measure sensitivity on real frames. Without this, no discovery claim is allowed.
- [ ] **Honest photometry.** Report aperture-dependence as a systematic error, use mid-exposure times, and calibrate the e⁻/ADU assumption for the Seestar.
- [ ] **Science-claim verification.** A result may only be stated as strongly as the evidence allows, enforced in code, so neither a template nor the LLM can overstate it.
  - *Claim levels* with explicit criteria, for example: `detected`, `consistent with known object`, `unidentified candidate`, `independently confirmed`. The mission log, upload-file notes and the dashboard show the level, and nothing can be promoted to `independently confirmed` automatically.
  - *Known-answer regression tests*: the archive replay (asteroid 8191) becomes committed tests with expected values and tolerances, so a pipeline change that shifts a result fails CI.
  - *Build a known-answer set for transits*: a deep, clean transit is the easy case. Add at least one shallower real transit and one real night with no transit, so the tests also cover "does it miss a weak signal" and "does it invent one". Expected values come from published parameters, with tolerances.
  - *Physical-model cross-check for transits*: compare the light curve with a limb-darkened transit model built from published parameters, in addition to the catalogue depth. Report duration on the same definition as the catalogue: a fixed 10 % ingress and a flat bottom read short on a limb-darkened transit, so fit a physical shape or label the number as a trapezoid duration.
  - *Injection-recovery and null fields on real frames*: measure sensitivity and the false-positive rate, and state them in the mission log and upload-file notes. Today this exists only on synthetic frames and as an ad-hoc check.
  - *Reproducibility record*: each result stores the code version, config and input data identifiers, so it can be re-derived.
  - *Human review gate*: nothing leaves the system (submission, publication) without a person signing off on the claim level.
- [x] **Remaining unattended safety (built and tested in simulation, 2026-11-14; not seen on the mount).** Free space where the frames are written must be at least `ASTRONOMO_MIN_FREE_GB` (default 2) or the capture does not start. A battery percent is used when the telescope reports one. Neither transport reports one today, and an unattended start refuses while the reading is missing. A person who confirms a start may go ahead without it.

### Phase 3: Bounded LLM decisions

**Goal:** give the model a small amount of real authority inside the deterministic graph, safely. The graph stays the orchestrator and safety spine (see [Decisions](decisions.md)).

- [ ] **Split the LLM call out of `generate_hypothesis`** into its own node, so it is replaceable, skippable and testable alone. Add per-node model config (`LLM_*` overrides) at the same time.
- [ ] `**propose_mission`.** Choose and order targets from the plugin's candidate list, before the observing loop starts. Schema-constrained output, validated in code, allowlist only, with the current priority sort as the fallback.
- [ ] **Experiment design.** Extend `propose_mission` so the model can also propose *how* to observe, not only what: exposure time, frame count and cadence, within bounds each plugin declares (for example min/max exposure, max frames, allowed cadence). Code validates every value against those bounds and falls back to the plugin's default plan. Choosing between plugins (which science case to run tonight) is a later extension once two plugins can share a night.
- [ ] `**evaluate_data`.** After `reduce_data`, decide accept / re-observe once / skip, from the digest only. Same validation and fallback (always accept), and the same budget and ledger machinery.
- [ ] **Proposed-mission view.** Add a `PROPOSED` status and a plan view (targets, order, rationale) in `vocab.lifecycle`, once `propose_mission` exists. Decide at that point whether a person approves a plan.

**Exit criteria:**

1. Both decision nodes run in real and simulated missions, with invalid or missing model output falling back to today's deterministic behaviour.
2. The model never calls hardware, and a test enforces that.
3. Per-node model selection works.
4. A proposed exposure plan outside a plugin's declared bounds is rejected and replaced by the plugin's default, and a test covers it.

### Phase 4: Close the loop

**Goal:** a finding is *tested*, and the system either stops with something worth reporting or reworks the experiment and goes round again, within hard limits. Requires Phase 2 (scheduler, claim levels) and Phase 3 (decision nodes).

- [ ] **Post-analysis decision node.** After cross-reference, choose one of a fixed set of actions: `done`, `follow_up` (observe the same target again), `redesign` (change the plan, for example a longer exposure or different cadence) or `drop` (give up on this target). Output is schema-constrained and validated; the fallback is `done`, which is today's behaviour.
- [ ] **Hard limits on the loop.** A maximum number of rounds per mission and per target, a total wall-clock and token budget, and a rule that the loop cannot lower any safety interlock. The loop ends in code, never because the model says so.
- [ ] **Confirmation observations.** The test of a finding is an independent re-observation: another night, another exposure setting, or another visit. A candidate is not promoted past `unidentified candidate` without one, and nothing reaches `independently confirmed` automatically (see the Phase 2 claim levels).
- [ ] **Rework record.** Store why each round happened (the finding, the decision, the new plan) so a person can audit it, and show the rounds on the mission page.
- [ ] **Loop-aware state.** The graph gains an edge from the decision node back to planning. Check checkpoint growth with multiple rounds, and keep each round's observations separate so a result can be traced to the round that produced it.

**Exit criteria:**

1. In simulation, a mission follows up a marginal candidate, confirms or rejects it on the second round, and stops at the round limit if it does not converge.
2. An invalid or missing model decision falls back to `done`.
3. A test enforces that the loop cannot bypass weather, Sun, Moon, window or altitude interlocks.
4. Every mission log lists the rounds and states the final claim level.

### Phase 5: Measure the models

**Goal:** answer "which model(s) should we be using?" with data. Requires Phase 3 (and benefits from Phase 4), because today there is nothing decision-shaped to compare.

- [ ] **Fixtures and scoring.** Expected-vs-chosen target order for `propose_mission`, accept/reject accuracy on labelled reductions for `evaluate_data`.
- [ ] **Same-task comparison.** Which LLM is best at a given task.
- [ ] **Cross-task comparison.** Whether one model works best as the orchestrating decision-maker while others handle subtasks.

**Exit criteria:** a written result that picks default models per node, with the fixtures committed so it can be repeated.
