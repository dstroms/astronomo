# Status: what is real and what is not yet

A snapshot of what Astronomo does today, what has been seen on a real telescope, and what has not. The plan is in the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md); the night-by-night evidence is in the [field journal](journal.md). Update this page when something here changes, and keep the roadmap and the README pointing here instead of repeating it.

Moved out of the roadmap on 2026-11-20 with the text unchanged, apart from the heading levels. **Phase numbers inside this page are the old ones** (Phases 1 to 6 before the reorganization); the mapping to the current phases is at the top of [decisions](decisions.md).

## The loop, stage by stage

The full scientific loop is the goal. A language model is optional, off by default, and never in charge of safety. The loop is reached through bounded decision nodes inside the deterministic graph, not by handing the model free control (see [Decisions](decisions.md)). Safety interlocks stay in code.

1. **Propose** missions and experiments (what to look at, and how to observe it).
2. **Set up** the telescope (connect, point, verify pointing, focus, calibrate).
3. **Run** the experiment.
4. **Collect** the data.
5. **Ingest and analyze** it.
6. **Determine findings.**
7. **Test the findings** (cross-check against catalogues, injection tests, independent confirmation observations).
8. **Submit** the findings through a real channel, **or rework** the experiment and go round again.

## What is real, and what is not yet (2026-11)

What Astronomo is today: a **deterministic simulation-proven pipeline** (fetch network targets, weather gate, observe, reduce, cross-reference, screen for anomalies, package upload files, write a mission log) with four science plugins (`variable_stars` and `transit_finder` are ready; `asteroid_finder` and `rubin_followup` are drafts, not ready for use, and are refused on a real telescope unless you opt in), a SQLite-backed dashboard and a LangGraph orchestrator that can resume after a crash. A language model is optional, off by default, and never in charge of safety. The full loop is the goal; the model joins it one bounded decision at a time. It has finished two supervised visits on a real Seestar. The magnitudes match Gaia G. The live upload file and a transit have not.

## What is real

- Full missions run end to end in simulation. Two tests skip because they need a real Ollama. The current pass count is the one in the [README](../README.md) status block.
- The output pipeline works in simulation for all four plugins: upload files with statuses (`simulated`, `replay`, `draft`, `ready_for_review`, `held`) and a manifest, a mission log, an anomaly screen with a threshold enforced in core, and an anomaly flagged in the mission log (the simulated Rubin outburst is flagged; the other runs are not). A variable-star visit writes one AAVSO Extended magnitude per star.
- The four live target lists have been fetched and parsed. ExoClock returned 776 planets on 2026-11-14 and every one parsed (WASP-12 b's period is 1.09142 d). Exoplanet Watch (3030 planets), the NASA TTV flag (497 names) and the MPC NEOCP (29 rows, RA in hours) were fetched on 2026-10-28. Trimmed copies are `tests/data/*_real.*`. The merged ranking is sensible (WASP-12 b, TTV-flagged, ranks high). An ExoClock O-C is kept only when that planet has a recent timing. Exoplanet Watch has a blank epoch uncertainty on 4 rows, a blank period uncertainty on 2, and an empty `host` on every row. An HTTP 200 error page used to be cached as data; `fetch_text(validate=...)` rejects it.
- The archive replay on real data works as a demonstration: ZTF asteroid visits (recovered a known mover that matched Horizons). The transit plugin has no real-data check yet.
- Weather, Sun altitude and missing-data gating fail safe, in code, outside the model's reach.
- Missions are checkpointed, observable in the dashboard, and budgeted for LLM tokens and calls.
- The dashboard has Home (every saved plan, then Needs attention / In progress / Completed), a mission page, an Opportunities page (what the networks want, what this site can reach in the next 30 days, a night calendar, a labelled impact estimate, and plans with a life cycle) and a Simple | Technical switch.
- **Site interlocks are built and rehearsed in simulation** (`core/interlocks.py`): observing window (wait before it opens, skip after it closes), minimum altitude, Sun separation and Moon separation scaled by illumination, on top of the weather and Sun-altitude gates. They apply on a real telescope, or in simulation with `ASTRONOMO_INTERLOCKS_IN_SIMULATION=1`. The Opportunities page uses the same limits and sky code, so a window it calls observable is one a mission would observe.
- **The observing site is a first-class setting** (`core/site.py`): set in the dashboard (place search, coordinates, an optional horizon) and saved as `site.json`, or by environment variable. Altitude, darkness, Sun, Moon and the Opportunities windows are all worked out for it, and a real-telescope mission refuses to start on the Denver placeholder.
- **A plan can be turned into a mission by a person**: `python main.py --from-plan ID` re-checks the plan (window, moved schedule, sky), builds a mission from exactly that target and follows it through `PLANNED`, `STARTED`, `DONE` or `MISSED`. With `--simulate` it is a rehearsal and leaves the plan alone. The Plans page and the rail list every saved plan, and a confirmed button on the Plans page runs that command. A real start from the button has not been used on the telescope.
- **Two variable-star visits have finished on a real Seestar** (2026-11-18, S50 Pro, firmware 9.75, Alpaca, Hat-P-23, plans #19 and #22, 12 frames each). Both missions reached `COMPLETE`. Frame 1 solved locally, one pointing correction put the target on the chip, and reanalysis measured 12.145 ± 0.057 and 12.139 ± 0.042 against Gaia G 12.162. The live AAVSO file was held. The 2026-11-13 capture (32 frames, link dropped, through a tree) and the 2026-11-15 HD 189733 visit (40 frames, star off the chip) do not count.

## What is not real yet

- **Upload files are checked against the published specifications, not against the destinations.** The AAVSO Exoplanet header was re-checked against the AAVSO's current published format (2026-10-28), which found a real mismatch: the old `Rflux` measurement type is gone and only `Rnflux` and `Dmag` are listed, so the writer now emits `Rnflux`, normalised to the out-of-transit level. It is still not validated by the AAVSO's own upload page (an empty `#DETREND_PARAMETERS=` in particular), and the upload also asks for a plate-solved image marking the target and comparison stars. The draft now attaches the first frame with those stars marked (2026-11-14). The marks are the pixels the light curve used, not a new plate solution, and a real Seestar frame has not been through it. ADES should be run through the MPC's validator. Observer, AAVSO and MPC station codes are not configured by default, so every real file is a `DRAFT_` until they are.
- **A 50 mm telescope is a poor match for most of the lists.** ExoClock asks for 5-inch-class apertures, so Astronomo uses its own V and depth limits; almost every NEOCP entry is too faint, so the asteroid plugin usually uses its built-in fallback; most Rubin alerts are fainter than 19.
- **The asteroid plugin proposes no anomalies**, and the Rubin plugin screens only for outbursts (not fading, not disappearance).
- **A clean known-answer night, and a transit, have not happened.** Two 12-frame Hat-P-23 visits finished on 2026-11-18 and the reanalyzed magnitudes match Gaia G. They used `ASTRONOMO_ASSUME_CLEAR`, and the live AAVSO file was held. The 2026-11-13 capture stopped at 32 frames. The 2026-11-15 visit took 40 frames of the wrong field. The solver that worked on 2026-11-18 is installed on this computer and is not packaged with Astronomo. The JSON-RPC driver is still tested only against a fake TCP server. The FITS-share path is unused over Alpaca. Still open in the [open hardware questions](first-light/05-first-light-checklist.md): the magnitude reached in one stack, the timing error, the interlocks against a planetarium, the Bayer pattern, `lastexposurestarttime` against a clock, the picture comparison on row 18, and the longitude sign on row 21. Focus, pointing, field rotation, and frame statistics have numbers from the Hat-P-23 visits. Filter selection and a plate solve on the scope are still open: Alpaca selects no filter and has no solve call.
- **Firmware 7.18 and newer needs a client-authentication handshake that the JSON-RPC transport does not implement.** The community reports an RSA challenge-response on port 4700 using a key taken from the vendor's app. Whether using that key is acceptable is a legal and ethical decision for the owner, so it is left out on purpose. **The route around it works for the calls a capture needs:** on firmware 9.75 the Alpaca server (port 32323, switched on in the app, no key) connected, unparked, slewed, exposed and returned uint16 ImageBytes. Still open: Bayer pattern, the timing source (`lastexposurestarttime`), filter selection, and a plate solve (Alpaca has no solve call today). Tracking has to be enabled after the slew; before it, the mount answered "below horizon". The reason the handshake is refused, and the firmware ranges the JSON-RPC transport handles, are in `[docs/first-light/03-firmware-and-authentication.md](first-light/03-firmware-and-authentication.md)`.
- **Safety is incomplete for unattended use.** The interlocks let a night-time capture start, and they have not been checked against a planetarium, so they are not proven. Dew (from the forecast's temperature and humidity), free space on this computer, and a battery percent when the telescope reports one are checked (2026-11-14, in simulation). The telescope does not report a battery, and an unattended start refuses while that reading is missing. Still not implemented: an *automatic* obstruction mask (a person can draw a horizon by hand in the site panel; nothing measures it) and a window ending *mid-sequence* (the window is checked when a target starts). None of the new checks have been seen on the mount.
- **Retries are built but unproven on real faults.** A node that fails with a transient network or link error is retried (`ASTRONOMO_NODE_RETRIES`, default 2, exponential backoff) and the telescope link reconnects and replays its set-up, but the policy has only met simulated faults. A device error (a reply code other than 0) is never retried.
- **Registration now corrects field rotation (2026-11-16), unproven on a clean real night.** The transit photometry registers each frame with a rotation about the frame centre plus a shift (`correct_rotation`), warning above `warn_rotation_edge_px` (1.5 px). With it off, registration is translation-only and refuses a light curve past `max_rotation_edge_px` (4 px). Both limits are guesses to be re-set from real frames. The first real night's 40 frames now give a light curve (they gave none before): the frames drifted about 400 px and rotated about 0.7 degrees, the stars were about 8.5 px wide (a wider finder is used when the 3 px one finds fewer than 8 stars), and the series was too short to fit a dip. The target and every comparison star faded together to about 55 percent of their first flux, and the differential curve kept a smooth trend of about 6 percent, which fits a 400 px drift across a field with no flat correction. On the next night the target was about 9 degrees from the zenith: the field rotated 1.8 degrees in 9 minutes and every star's flux swung by up to 35 percent between frames (the weather lookup had failed). A maximum altitude (default 70 degrees) now refuses that. On 2026-11-18 the Hat-P-23 visits rotated 1.64° and 0.84°, and the reduction corrected both. The limits are still guesses.
- **"Autonomous" means the pipeline acts without a human, not that a model reasons.** The LLM writes one advisory prose piece (the hypothesis rationale) and controls nothing. Target choice and ordering come from the plugin and a priority sort.
- **No discovery claims are supportable.** "Unidentified" means "not explained by the objects queried", never "new". SkyBoT and MPC are not consulted, archive visits are not linked across nights, and sensitivity has only been measured on synthetic frames.
- **Real-data results are demonstrations, not measurements.** Photometry is instrumental, depth depends on aperture, and there is no quoted systematic error.
- **The scheduler does not start a night by itself.** `python main.py --schedule` lists due plans and resumes a mission whose weather wait has passed. A plan starts only with `--confirm ID`. Self-start (`ASTRONOMO_SELF_START`) is off, and is refused unless `ASTRONOMO_UNATTENDED_CONFIRMED` is also set, which is the record that the interlocks and parking have been seen on this mount. Nothing in the repository sets that flag. A sleeping archive replay still holds a process.
- **Weather waits, and then the process is handed back.** If weather blocks a target past `max_sleep_cycles`, the mission keeps a wake time and exits, instead of aborting. The scheduler resumes it. A saved forecast's next clear hour is the wake time when the file has one. Site limits that never clear (a target that never rises) still end the mission.
- **Rubin follow-up has not been run live.** The alert stream was dark when this was written (expected back around mid/late October 2026; not re-checked), and a 50 mm telescope reaches roughly mag 15-16 while most alerts are fainter.
- **The LLM surface is too thin to benchmark.** Two prose tasks do not discriminate between models.
